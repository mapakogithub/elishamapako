About

I'm a geospatial scientist passionate about applying GIS, remote sensing and spatial data management to support wildlife conservation and landscape-level decision-making. Having contributed to field-based conservation monitoring and geodatabase management during my internship with the Zimbabwe Parks and Wildlife Authority (ZIMPARKS), I'm drawn to science-led, community-inclusive conservation work.

I bring a strong technical foundation in ArcGIS, QGIS, SMART and Google Earth Engine, combined with hands-on experience in mobile data collection, Web GIS development and geospatial storytelling — ready to support accurate, accessible and impactful spatial data across the landscapes I serve.

Research Student — BSc (Hons) Dissertation

University of Zimbabwe · GIS & Earth Observation · Final-year research project · 2026
  • Analysed GPS telemetry from ten collared elephants (about 44,700 fixes, 2015–2018) across Hwange National Park and the KAZA-TFCA.
  • Extracted season-matched MODIS NDVI and land-surface temperature at every GPS fix using Google Earth Engine.
  • Modelled movement against temperature and vegetation with linear mixed models in R, and measured site fidelity with grid-cell persistence and Jaccard similarity.

Research Publications

PublishedBook chapter · 2026

Future of Climate-Resilient Agriculture Using Artificial Intelligence

Olipa N. Lungu, Elisha Mapako, Joseph O. Iyanda, Emmanuel B. Effa, Never Mujere

Computational Intelligence for Climate Change, pp. 435–450, 2026

Reviews how artificial intelligence, including remote sensing, modelling, precision agriculture, machine learning and the Internet of Things, supports productive and sustainable farming under climate change. It traces how crop production, soil and water management and monitoring have changed with AI worldwide, looks at the barriers smallholder farmers face in adopting these tools, and sets out opportunities for support that contribute to the Sustainable Development Goals, especially SDG 2 (Zero Hunger).

Climate-resilient agricultureRemote sensingPrecision agricultureMachine learningSDG 2
In preparationResearch manuscript · from BSc dissertation

Seasonal movement dynamics of African elephants Loxodonta africana in relation to temperature, vegetation and spatial memory in Hwange National Park

The second publication from the dissertation. It examines how ten GPS-collared elephants move through Hwange National Park and the wider KAZA landscape across the dry and wet seasons, and how that movement relates to temperature, vegetation greenness (NDVI) and spatial memory.
